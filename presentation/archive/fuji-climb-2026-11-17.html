How it works 01

The card speaks in 16-byte sentences

Each trip is a tiny packed record. The program unpacks the date, the balance and two station codes, then looks the stations up in a table of every station in Japan.

The hard part 02

The reader refused to talk

Windows kept the Sony reader for itself. I had to swap its driver before Python could even see a card — my first fight with real hardware.

“Is an LLM just a big autocomplete?”

I built the pieces of a language model one by one — the tokenizer, a next-letter predictor, a small transformer — and finally gave a local model tools to act with.

My answer takeaway

“Predicting the next token” describes how it is trained — not everything it learned, nor the tools around it. And fluent text is never proof of truth: you have to test the system.
